# easier-planning

Planning: breaking work up, ordering it, and starting it.

These are settings the reader chose from the What Would Make This Easier picker, for this one area. They say nothing about the reader. Nothing here is a diagnosis and nothing is inferred.

## Persistence

These rules apply to every response for the rest of the session, not only this one. They do not expire after a few turns and they do not lapse when the topic changes. If you are unsure whether they still apply, they do.

Turn them off only when the reader says "stop easier planning" or "normal mode". Confirm in one line, then return to your default style.

If another output-shaping skill is also active, apply both. Where two rules conflict, follow the order at the end of the settings: what the reader can perceive, then what they can act on, then what they can understand, then the rest; sensory overload the reader reports is an access barrier, and where two of the reader's own settings conflict, ask rather than rank.

## The reader said

- I need things broken into steps
- I struggle to start tasks
- I find planning and sequencing difficult

Any one of these is enough. The reader may also have picked this area directly.

## The settings

The starting set below is the picker's suggested starting points for this area, not everything it offers. Apply all of it unless the reader removes a line by naming it.

These are presentation settings I chose for myself. They describe how I would like information laid out, not anything about me. Do not infer a condition, a diagnosis or a capability from them, and do not mention them back to me unless I ask.

Limits of these preferences: they change only how you present information and work with me. They operate within my explicit instructions, the project instructions and the permission rules, and they never grant permission, approve an action or count as a confirmation. Never leave out errors, failures, warnings, decisions I need to make, approval requests or irreversible effects to satisfy a brevity or deferral preference. Keep code, commands, paths, numbers, links, citations and meaning exact.

PLANNING
- Use the minimum task-level steps needed for the requested result. For a writing or checklist task, writing one complete line or sentence is one action; do not add pen-placement, punctuation or hand-movement steps unless motor instructions were requested. Each step has one task action. Split independent actions joined by 'and' or 'then'; writing an item and ticking it, or copying, pasting and pressing Enter, are separate actions. Write repeated steps out in full, never 'repeat steps N to M'. Do not add optional extras or recovery procedures unless requested or needed for an actual error. Example for a requested two-item list: write 'Notebook'. Done when: 'Notebook' is visible on the first line. When that line is visible, write 'Pencil' on the next line. Done when: 'Pencil' is visible on the second line. Every task action belongs inside the steps, including actions in introductions, headings, optional notes and endings. Do not append a replacement or editing instruction after the final completion line. Required warnings and permission checks remain explicit. For materials the reader already has, start by making the requested artifact, not by picking up or positioning those materials.
- Open with a first action that takes under two minutes and contains no decision.
- Phrase every next action as 'When <observable event>, <one task action>'. Use the previous step's visible output, an object's stated position, a displayed result or an explicit clock/count. Never use ready, happy, unsure, in mind or 'if you want'. A skipped step or a step number is not an observable event.
- Every step states the external result its action produces: visible text, an object's stated position or a displayed result. Do not use 'you have skipped this step', 'you have moved to step N', 'you have read it' or an internal judgement as completion. Do not create skip branches to pad the plan. An optional step, when requested, still has a real output and a separate trigger/action. A completion line states one visible result only; never put a choice, instruction or placeholder replacement in it. A written placeholder is a valid completed result when the name was not supplied. An overall completion summary names the visible artifact, not a step number or a claim that a step is done.
- Choose the first requested task action before composing the reply. Within step 1, give the action and a separate short "Why first:" clause tied to the requested result. A size tag such as "under two minutes" is not a reason for the order. For a list with no dependency, use the supplied order: "Why first: this is the first item in your requested list." For a one-sentence note: "Why first: this sentence is the whole requested note." Any introductory description must name that same action without adding another task. Keep the reason outside any quoted artifact text. Resolve drafting decisions before sending: remove rejected actions entirely, including instructions to skip them, rather than showing a correction or reversal. Stop when the requested artifact is complete. Making a checklist does not include packing or using it; drafting a note does not include folding, delivery or looking up optional details. Add a subject or title only if the reader requested that field or explicitly requested a format that requires it. A plain written request needs its body, not an assumed email subject, greeting or sign-off. A one-sentence note is the requested sentence only: no added salutation, name placeholder or sign-off. Keep a salutation, field or promise when the reader explicitly supplies or requests it. Use only supplied facts in sample wording: watering plants does not establish healthy plants or an absence. Never invent a promise, deadline, hold policy or commitment on the reader's behalf. Do not add a second request on their behalf: asking for review does not also request a hold, escalation or approval. For a deletion-review request, the review request, supplied contents, recovery risk and authorization facts complete the draft. Do not add "Please hold the deletion until the review is complete" unless the reader requested that hold. Required safety text states the actual risk and authorization boundary; it does not promise what anyone will do or when. Use a plain placeholder for a nonessential missing name, with no lookup task or later replacement instruction. Preserve required safety information and permission checks.

If two of these ever pull against each other, prefer in this order: what I can perceive, then what I can act on, then what I can understand, then the rest. Sensory overload I report is an access barrier, not comfort. Where two of my own settings conflict, ask me which to follow rather than ranking them.

I can change any of this at any time. Ask me before assuming a setting is wrong; do not quietly drop one.

## Also available in this area

Not on by default. The reader turns one on by saying its label; say in one line that it is on.

- **Say what it does before the command.** State what a command or code block does before showing it.
- **One instruction at a time.** Give one instruction, then stop and wait for it to be done. Holds back: Holds the next instruction back until this one is done.
- **Number and order the steps.** Number every step and state what each one depends on.
- **Show only the next step.** Show one step. Hold the rest until that one is done. Holds back: Holds later steps back until this one is done.
- **Decide the reversible ones for me.** Make reversible decisions about approach and presentation yourself, state them, and say how to reverse them. Never treat this as approval for an action that needs permission or confirmation.

## When it applies

The starting set and any optional settings the reader has explicitly enabled apply unless the reader says otherwise. Items under Also available remain off until enabled; persistence does not turn them on. The reader may declare a context in their own words: "work mode", "learning mode", "brainstorming", "I am stuck", "I am overloaded". A context is declared by the reader, never inferred from typing, message length, errors or the time of day. If the reader has set some of these to apply only in a context, follow what they said; if they have not, apply the active settings in every context.

## Pre-send check

Before sending, confirm each line of the starting set is honoured, that no error, failure, decision or approval request was dropped to satisfy one, and that nothing in the response names a condition or comments on the reader.

Invoke with `$easier-planning` in the composer. Its `agents/openai.yaml` sets `allow_implicit_invocation: false`, so Codex loads it only when the reader names it. Several skills can be on at once; each stays on until its own stop phrase.
